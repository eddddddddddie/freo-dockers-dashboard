# Coach View: coach and analyst sessions

A script for 30-minute sessions with 2 or 3 coaches or analysts. The goal is to see where the
Coach View helps, where people hesitate or look in the wrong place, and what they want that it
doesn't show. Watch and take notes; don't teach the app during the tasks.

## Before the session

- One person per session, with you beside them. 30 minutes: 2 intro, 20 tasks, 8 debrief.
- Their own laptop or the screen they would really use (a laptop, or the TV in the coaches' box).
- Sign them in, then start the tour with the ? button at the top right. Let them go
  through it or skip it, and note which.
- Open the Wharf-ai usage log afterwards (Deep dives, then Wharf-ai usage): what they asked is
  part of the result.
- Ask if you can take notes. Say it is the app being tested, not them.

## Say at the start

"This is a draft of a performance dashboard. I'll give you five things to find out. Think out
loud as you go: what you're looking for, what you expect to see, anything that surprises you.
There are no wrong answers. If you'd normally ask someone or give up, say so."

## The five tasks

Time each task from when you finish reading it to when they give an answer. Stop at 4 minutes.
Don't help unless they're stuck for a minute; if you do, note it.

| # | Task (read it out) | Correct answer (2026) | Where it is |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "How did the season go, in one sentence?" | 21-6, minor premiers on 19-4 (137.2%), lost the grand final by 7. | Season band; Scout for the ladder |
| 2 | "Why did we lose the grand final?" | Kicked 12.17 (41.4%) to Brisbane's 14.12 (53.8%) from 3 more scoring shots; won inside 50s by 14 and clearances by 14; led by 10 at three quarter time. | Match view (click GF in the game strip) |
| 3 | "Is Caleb Serong in form?" | 24.5 disposals a game in 2026, 1st in the squad for contested possessions and clearances, down about 10% on 2025. Last 5 against his average is on his Player view. | Player view (from Player form, or the picker) |
| 4 | "We play Brisbane next. What do we need to know?" | 3rd, 16-7 (121.7%); top 3 for inside 50s and clearances; win when they win metres gained (94%). Freo 1-2 against them. | Scout view |
| 5 | "The board wants to know one thing that decides our games. What would you tell them?" | Metres gained differential moves with the margin most (r 0.89); inside 50s next. Association, not cause. | Season: What drives our margin; or Wharf-ai |

After tasks 2 and 4, ask: "How sure are you of that answer? Would you say it to the senior
coach?" (1 to 5).

## What to watch for

- Where they look first. Do they read the takeaway line under each card title, or the chart?
- Whether they find the views (Season, Match, Player, Scout) and the Deep dives menu unprompted.
- Whether they try to click a chart. Do they find that clicking a game or a player opens it?
- Hover: do they discover it? On a touch screen, what happens when they tap?
- Wharf-ai: do they use it, and when? Do they trust its numbers? Do they use the follow-ups?
- Words they use that the app doesn't ("stoppage", "structure", "possession chain").
- Anything they want that isn't in the data (shot locations, zones, positions). The app can't
  show these; note how often they come up.

## Note sheet (one per task)

```
Task #   Time (s)   Answer right? (Y / partly / N)   Helped? (Y/N)
Path taken:
Hesitated at:
Said:
Confidence (1-5):
```

## Debrief questions (8 minutes)

1. What would you use this for in a normal week? When would you open it?
2. What was the most useful thing on the screen? The least?
3. What did you look for that wasn't there?
4. Where would you use it: desk, coaches' box, the rooms, a phone?
5. If you could change one thing, what would it be?

## Afterwards

- Put each person's times and answers side by side. A task that more than one person got wrong
  or took over 2 minutes is a design problem, not a user problem.
- List every "looked for but wasn't there" and every Wharf-ai question. Recurring ones are
  candidates for a card or a view.
- Group the problems: finding things (navigation), reading things (layout, labels), trusting
  things (numbers, explanations), missing things (data or features).
- Re-run the same five tasks after the next round of changes, and compare the times.
